The AI Usage and Impact on Students & Professionals dataset focuses on understanding how people use Artificial Intelligence tools in their education, work, and daily activities. The dataset includes both students and professionals, allowing us to compare their AI usage patterns and understand how AI affects different groups of users. It contains demographic information such as age, gender, country, education level, and profession, along with information about income and the type of user.
The dataset also provides detailed information about AI usage behavior. It records which AI tools users prefer, such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, and other AI platforms. It also identifies the main purpose for which AI is used, including studying, coding, writing, research, data analysis, content creation, office work, and customer support. The AI_Usage_Hours_Per_Day feature helps measure how much time users spend using AI each day, while Tasks_Performed shows the types of activities they complete with AI assistance.
Another important part of the dataset is measuring the impact of AI on users. Features such as Productivity_Score, Accuracy_Rating, and Satisfaction_Score help determine how users feel AI affects their work or studies. The Time_Saved_Hours_Per_Week feature can be used to investigate whether AI helps users save time. AI_Experience_Years can also help analyze whether users with more experience using AI achieve different productivity or satisfaction levels compared with beginners.
The dataset can be used to perform several interesting analyses. For example, we can investigate whether professionals use AI more frequently than students, which AI tools are most popular, which professions depend more heavily on AI, and which purposes have the highest usage. We can also examine whether increased AI usage is associated with higher productivity, greater satisfaction, or more time saved. Comparisons can be made between countries, education levels, professions, and different experience levels.
From a Data Science perspective, this dataset is useful because it contains both numerical and categorical variables. Numerical features such as age, income, usage hours, productivity score, satisfaction score, and time saved can be analyzed statistically, while categorical features such as gender, country, profession, AI tool, and AI purpose can be analyzed using grouping and encoding techniques.
It is also suitable for Feature Engineering. New features could be created, such as Weekly_AI_Usage_Hours, Productivity_Per_Hour, High_AI_User, or AI_Cost_Per_Usage_Hour. These engineered features can provide additional insights and can later be used as inputs for machine-learning models.
Overall, this dataset provides a practical way to study the adoption, usage, and impact of Artificial Intelligence among students and professionals. It can take you through a complete Data Science workflow, starting from data inspection and cleaning, followed by EDA, visualization, feature engineering, statistical analysis, and eventually machine learning.
